# <a name="regulatoryE"></a>Regulatory element annotation for transcribed and non-transcribed CREs 

To characterize the regulatory landscape of the identified CREs, we analyzed GC content, CpG islands (CGIs), and core promoter elements. The 5-base GC content and CGI annotation from hg38 were retrieved from UCSC. For GC content profiling, mid-points of CREs were extended 2 kb up- and down-stream and intersected with the 5-base GC content annotation. A null background model was generated by randomly sampling genomic regions matching the total number of CREs, masking all the exons from GENCODE v39 and all the identified CREs. For CGI, the whole region of CREs showing > 200 nt overlap with CGI annotation were considered CGI-positive.
The motifs of the TATA box (TBP: MA0108.3) and the initiator (INR: POL002.1) were obtained from the Jaspar database. Genome wide motif occurrences were mapped using scanMotifGenomeWide.pl of Homer (v4.11).11 For transcribed CREs, TATA box and INR motifs were searched 50 bp up-stream centered on the TSS summits. If there is more than one occurrence in the 50 nt region, the ones with higher motif scores were taken. A high-confidence TATA-box was defined by a motif score at least 3 located between -34 to -27 nt from the first base of the TATA motif relative to the TSS. 

# <a name="TATApredict"></a>TATA box prediction from non-transcribed CREs 

To identify potential TATA box and Inr occurrences for the non-transcribed CREs, we scanned a 501 nt window relative to the midpoints of all CREs. We required a TATA-box (score ≥ 3) and an INR (score ≥ 48.5) located from 10-45 bp of each other. Inr motif coordinates were intersected with TSS to define positive TATA-Inr pairs that support transcription. The preferred TATA-Inr spacing was determined using logistic regression, with TATA motif score, Inr motif score, and TATA–Inr distance as predictors where distance was modeled using a natural cubic spline with three degrees of freedom. The optimal spacing interval was defined as distances for which the fitted probability was at least 95% of the maximum predicted probability, yielding a TATA-Inr spacing of 24–33 bp. Candidate TATA-Inr pairs were subsequently restricted to 24–33 bp and collapsed to retain the highest-scoring TATA motif per Inr. The resulting data were randomly divided into training (70%) and test (30%) sets. A binomial logistic regression model was fitted to the training data using TATA and Inr motif scores as predictors of transcriptional support. The fitted linear predictor was used as the combined TATA-Inr score. Model discrimination was evaluated on the held-out test set using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis. For subsequent prediction of TATA-associated CREs lacking detectable transcription, a score threshold corresponding to ≥95% specificity was selected from the ROC curve and applied together with the 24–33-bp TATA–Inr spacing requirement.

# <a name="enrichment"></a>Enrichment of chromatin states with transcription and regulatory elements 

Enrichment of transcription and regulatory sequence features across cell-type-specific chromatin states was assessed separately for promoter- and enhancer-like CREs. Analyses were restricted to CREs accessible in the corresponding cell type. CREs were classified as transcribed when at least one confident TSS count was detected within the CRE and as non-transcribed otherwise. Regulatory sequence features were classified as CpG island (CGI), TATA-box or null.
Enrichment was assessed using Fisher’s exact test based on 2 × 2 contingency tables. For example, among accessible enhancer-like CREs in iPSCs (n = 186,074), chromatin states were grouped as active versus non-active (primed or repressed) and compared with transcribed versus non-transcribed CREs to calculate odds ratios and P values. For regulatory sequence features, accessible and transcribed enhancer-like CREs in iPSCs (n = 15,543) were similarly grouped by chromatin state and tested for enrichment of individual features. For CGI enrichment, CREs were classified as CGI-positive versus CGI-negative, with the latter comprising TATA-box and null CREs. Equivalent analyses were performed for the other cell types, chromatin states and regulatory sequence features.

# <a name="gcoordinate"></a>Genomic coordinate of CREs 

Coordinates of CREs were intersected with coordinates of annotated transcript models from GENCODE v39 without considering strand. For CREs overlapping multiple genomic features, a representative feature was selected based on a prioritized hierarchy: 1) 5’end (TSS), 2) 5’UTR, 3) 3’UTR, 4) exon, and 5) intron. CREs without any overlap were considered as intergenic.
